Lesson 6: TestClock — time moves only when the test says, and adjust waits for the fallout

TestClock.layer() provides a Clock whose sleep parks the fiber on a latch keyed by a deadline, and a Scheduler that counts the work a wake causes. adjust(d) moves the clock past due deadlines one at a time and waits until the work those wakes scheduled has run, or the busy-warning timeout expires, so the test asserts straight after it, with no extra yields.

1 · sleep(d)

Insert (now + d, latch) into a descending list, before equal deadlines (first sleeper wakes first). Start the one-per-clock warning fiber, then wait on the latch. Interrupted: the entry is removed.

2 · adjust: barrier

Under the run semaphore, fork yield_now and await it, so fibers already scheduled get to register their sleeps first. Stop the warning.

3 · adjust: wake each due sleep

Pop the earliest due entry, advance the clock to its deadline (monotonic rounds half up, wall floors), open its latch with tracking on. Every task the wake schedules is counted.

4 · settle

Count zero: yield once. Otherwise wait for the count to drain; if it doesn't within warning_delay of live time, print the busy warning on the live console. Then advance to the end time.

Why the warnings go to the live clock and console. A test that forgot to adjust would never see a warning timed by the TestClock, and a TestConsole would swallow it. So both warnings use with_live and the Console captured when the clock was made.
Python adaptations. current_time_millis is a float (a TestClock can stand at 0.0015 ms). The TestClock and TestConsole namespaces are instances, not classes, so pytest does not collect them as test classes. test_clock_with on a live clock is a TypeError defect where upstream casts.
How we know it holds. All 25 TestClock.test.ts cases and S1–S14 in tests/test_testclock.py, plus a production mutation run over the TestClock, logger, console and fork_scoped sources. Rows TC1–TC6 in docs/INVARIANTS.md.
